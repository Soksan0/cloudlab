# CloudLab release review — 26 November 2026

This is an agent-driven review, not evidence of comprehension by real students. The ordinary-UI endurance session uses an isolated browser company, no injected funds or company state, the guided setup, live requests, purchases, prerequisites, claims, incidents, and all three views. The deterministic systems and developer scenarios use isolated fixtures deliberately.

## Four review perspectives

- **New student:** ordinary-UI introduction and guided setup, exact CPU and purchase targets, monitor diagnosis, Memory/Storage, separate reward claim, and the first encounter. Fresh sandbox benchmarks also measure the first successful upgrade. The 8/20-second reminders and reduced-motion cues have independent browser checks.
- **Game reviewer:** a recorded endurance session continues through contained traffic waves and failed hardware incidents, observes real gains and losses, claims rewards, follows prerequisites, and switches scenes. A poor purchasing strategy can still overbuild or neglect redundancy; the UI labels headroom rather than inventing earnings. No bankruptcy/reset is added.
- **Systems reviewer:** isolated coverage exercises offered pressure versus actual use, owned versus remote resources, shared storage and full-disk writes, cache eligibility, ingress limits, failover, rental startup, recurring costs, and intervention previews. The model does not claim real measured queues, per-core imbalance, watts, or percentile latency.
- **Developer:** save/import/reload and migration, event boundaries at 1×/2×/4×, hidden/paused clocks, duplicate claims, rapid input, transition cancellation, keyboard controls, policy navigation, reduced motion, bounded histories/receipts/listeners, and sustained 4× rendering. Results and final counts are recorded in the handoff after the final gate.

## Evidence and limits

The tracked endurance summary is produced from `benchmarks/play-review.mjs`; transient screenshots live in ignored `test-results/`. Its automatic choices are a reproducible review aid, not a human usability study. In particular, repeated selection of the highlighted limiting part can be a poor strategy during an availability incident. The corrected debrief now teaches the relevant protection instead.

Financial and economy claims use engine ledgers. Sampled browser graphs and request tickets are not packet captures or distinct customer counts. The balance agent uses one deterministic seed and explicit 0/5/10-second decision delays; it cannot establish how long a student takes to read or understand a card.

Design references: [Microsoft XAG 109](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/109) supports reviewable objectives and directional help when progress stops; [Factorio FFF 280](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-280) demonstrates visible consequences and manual camera exploration. [AWS scaling limits](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/autoscaling/ec2/userguide/asg-capacity-limits.html) distinguishes configured minimum/maximum capacity from automatic desired capacity. CloudLab's upfront deployment fees remain fictional, not an AWS billing claim.
